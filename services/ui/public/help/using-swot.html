SWOT analysis and response planning

Use ISMS → SWOT analysis to record strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats, explain their significance and plan a response. The SWOT analysis tab on the ISMS page shows read-only quadrants; use Edit SWOT analysis to open the planning workspace at /swot.html. Dates are optional for every quadrant, including strengths. Carry out these tasks in your organisation’s KEEN workspace.

SWOT captures context and choices. Risks hold assessed scenarios, likelihood, impact and treatment. Objectives hold measurable outcomes and the work needed to achieve them. Link these records so reviewers can follow the reasoning from an issue to an action and its evaluation.

QuadrantWhat to considerExample response
StrengthAn existing capability that supports the organisation, such as an experienced incident response team.Maintain skills through exercises and share expertise.
WeaknessAn internal limitation, such as dependence on one person for recovery procedures.Document the procedure and train a second operator.
OpportunityA favourable possibility, such as automating repeated assurance checks.Pilot automation and evaluate coverage and time saved.
ThreatA condition that could undermine an outcome, such as widespread staff absence.Review continuity arrangements and test critical cover.

Create a useful SWOT item

  1. Choose the framework you are working with, open SWOT analysis and select New SWOT item. Each quadrant also has a New item button that preselects its type.
  2. Enter a short Title and select the Quadrant. Use one record for each issue that needs its own owner, response or evaluation.
  3. Choose Significance (1 low – 5 high). In Context and significance rationale, explain the affected activities, supporting observations and why the rating is appropriate.
  4. Set an Accountable owner, Status, optional Planned start and Target completion. The target must be on or after the start.
  5. Describe the intended action, resources, success criteria and review approach in Planned response and evaluation. Use a linked objective when the response needs a measurable target.
  6. Select a Parent item if this is part of a broader issue. Add relevant control and clause references for the selected framework, then open Related items to add any supporting records before saving.
  7. Move between Details, Related items and Visualisation while preparing the draft. Select Save SWOT item and relationships to create the item, mappings and relationships together. A failed save retains the draft so you can correct it. Questions and audit sampling become available after the first save.

Agree how to rate significance

The rating is a whole number from 1 to 5. Agree a consistent interpretation within your organisation. For example, use 1 for a limited local effect, 3 for an issue needing coordinated attention and 5 for an issue central to a strategic or continuity outcome. These are suggested criteria; record your own rationale. The rating is independent of a linked risk’s likelihood and impact.

Review the rating when circumstances change. A completed response can leave an important strength or threat relevant to future planning. Explain the current position in the item and use the changelog to follow changes.

Group issues into a hierarchy

Create the parent first, then select it in each child’s Parent item field. For example, create a Threat called “Pandemic”, with children “Sickness / staff absenteeism” and “Supply chain issues”. Each child can have a separate owner, rating, status, dates and related records.

Parents and children share a quadrant. Hierarchies support up to 12 levels and reject circular relationships. Ratings and statuses belong to individual records; evaluate parent progress explicitly. To move an item to another quadrant, first resolve any parent or child relationships that would cross quadrants. Choose Top level to remove its parent.

Connect frameworks and related records

In Details & response, select catalogue entries under Controls in selected framework and Clauses in selected framework. Use Ctrl (Command on macOS) or Shift to select multiple entries. Save, then check the displayed links. Select another framework to manage its mappings; mappings are retained separately for each framework.

In Related items, choose an Entity type, enter search text, select Search, choose the Record and select Link item. Use Unlink to remove an association while retaining the related record. Searches return a limited set of choices; narrow the search if needed.

Related items are grouped into compact cards by type, with a count for each group. On a new item, Link item and Unlink change the draft only; nothing is written until the SWOT item is saved. On an existing item, relationship actions save immediately. Up to 200 relationships are supported.

Related recordWhy link it?
ObjectiveDescribe a measurable response and its evaluation.

Follow the SWOT tab on supported related record detail pages to return to the analysis. People and vendors expose SWOT links from their records, and control and clause pages show mapped SWOT items in their ISMS information. Associations provide navigation and context; record the actual response and evaluation in the appropriate records.

Choose a view and filter the analysis

Expand the collapsed Filters section. Use Search for title or context, and combine Type, Significance, Status, control or clause selections, and Related to with a Related item. Filters update as you select values or pause typing; Apply filters also refreshes the results. The Related item selector appears when the chosen type has records available. Clearing Related to clears and hides that selector. Significance selects an exact rating. Framework references apply to the selected framework. Clear selections and apply again to widen the view.

Views show the current page of filtered results. Use Previous and Next to review the remaining items. The workspace loads up to 100 records per page, so a board or timeline is a view of that page rather than a whole-register total.

Track the response through its stages

The shared statuses are Defined, Planned, In progress, Under review, Completed, Deferred and Superseded. Agree who changes status and what evidence each stage needs. A useful practice is to move to Under review when action is finished, then Completed after checking the outcome. Explain postponement or replacement in the response text and link any replacement work where appropriate.

Turn SWOT responses into measurable objectives

Open ISMS → Objectives to create an objective. Record its requirement, goal, owner, metric, resource users and resource requirements. Set planned start and target completion dates. Describe the Completion method and Evaluation method, then save and link the objective from the SWOT item’s Related items tab.

Open ISMS → Objectives and choose Table, Status board or Timeline in Objectives view. These are three views of the same objective records. The Objectives link from SWOT analysis opens this tab. Use the shared search and status filters to focus any view; changes to an objective appear in all three. Existing Objective planning bookmarks open the Status board here. Open a card to inspect the objective. Update status using the board’s drag-and-drop or Status selector. Dates and statuses are maintained explicitly; a linked SWOT item keeps its own dates and status.

Worked example: under “Pandemic”, give “Sickness / staff absenteeism” a significance of 4 with an explanation of critical service dependencies. Link the relevant service asset, continuity process and assessed risk. Create an objective: “By 30 September, two trained operators can perform each critical recovery procedure”. Specify the people and exercise time needed, how training is completed, and how an observed exercise checks successful recovery. Link that objective to the SWOT item and retain the exercise results in the appropriate evidence records. Review the results before marking the response completed.

For planning around ISO 27001 clauses 6.1 and 6.2, record the issue, planned action, integration into relevant processes and approach to evaluating effectiveness. Use objectives to make the intended result, responsibility, resources, timing and evaluation explicit. Map the applicable clauses from your installed framework and review supporting evidence. A mapping records relevance; assess fulfilment against your organisation’s requirements.

Ask questions and include SWOT in audits

Open an item’s Questions tab to read its discussion or select Ask question after entering your question. Ask for the basis of a rating, the response owner’s decision or evidence that the intended result is achieved. Reply controls depend on your thread permissions.

Use Sample into audit on the item to select an audit you can manage and record the sample through the audit dialog. To include SWOT in the planned coverage, select the relevant SWOT items when editing the audit scope. Scope identifies what the audit examines; samples identify records chosen for examination. Review both on the audit. See Plan audits and sample evidence for the wider workflow.

Selected SWOT titles and quadrants are shared with readers of the audit. Consider that audience when choosing scope. Access to related records, including risks, remains subject to their permissions. Complete scope and sample changes while the audit is editable; Completed and Archived audits restrict changes.

Export SWOT analysis

Use Export Excel or Export PDF above the workspace views. Exports include all items matching the current filters, across result pages, with mappings from the selected framework and related records you can read. Changelog entries and question threads are excluded.

The Excel workbook has separate Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats worksheets. Each item has columns for its attributes, owner, hierarchy parent ID, framework controls and clauses, and each relationship type. Related names and IDs are grouped in their respective cells; very long values continue in additional columns. Headers are frozen and filterable. PDF presents the same attributes in a paginated report grouped by type.

Exports support up to 500 items and 10 MB of source text; PDF is limited to 150 pages. Narrow the filters if a limit is reached. Use Cancel export to stop preparation. An unavailable record or failed request stops the export without downloading a partial file. Records are read during preparation, so avoid concurrent editing when you need a consistent report.

Review history, access and editing problems

The Changelog tab records changes to the SWOT item and its associations. Relationship-change entries omit target identities and titles to preserve the linked records’ access boundaries. Consult the current Related items tab for associations you can read.

  • Edit or sample controls are unavailable: ask your administrator to check the relevant ISMS, question or audit permissions. Being named as an owner does not grant application permissions.
  • A linked record is missing: it may have been removed or you may lack access. Link visibility follows the related record’s access rules.
  • Someone else changed the item: reload it, review the latest values and reconcile your changes before saving again.
  • An item is absent from a chart: check filters, pagination and target dates. A timeline needs a target completion date.
  • Deletion is blocked: resolve child items and audit-scope references first. Consider retaining the record with Deferred or Superseded status when its history is useful.
